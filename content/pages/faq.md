<h2 id="cash-vs-listing">Cash offer vs listing with an agent</h2>
<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Should I take a cash offer or list my house with an agent?</summary><p>You do not have to pick before we talk. We show a cash offer and a market-sale option, and we compare both with a traditional listing.</p><p>Cash is often 10–14 business days. A market sale is usually 45–90 days. A traditional listing is often 3–6 months or more, often about 6% commission, and the seller typically pays some closing costs. Those are typical industry figures. On our two paths, commission is none and we cover closing costs.</p></details>
<details><summary>What's the difference between your cash offer and your market-sale option?</summary><p>A cash offer is us buying the house. The market-sale option takes longer and can net more. We do not charge a commission or closing costs on that path either.</p><p>Cash is usually 10–14 business days, and a market sale is usually 45–90 days. <details><summary>Why is a cash offer lower than what my house might list for?</summary><p>A cash price is usually below a list price because it has to cover repairs, carrying the house, and the chance it takes time to sell again.</p><p>A person reviews the property, not an automatic calculator, and this page will not quote a percent of retail. If cash is not your best result, the market-sale option may put more in your pocket, on a slower clock of about 45–90 days.</p></details>

<details><summary>How do I compare your offer to what I'd net with an agent?</summary><p>Compare what you would receive, not the headline price.</p><p>Line up price, commission, closing costs, repairs, and any payoff the title company sends to a lender. On our cash offer and our market-sale path, commission is none and we cover closing costs. A traditional listing often includes about 6% commission and some seller closing costs. Those are typical industry figures, not a quote.</p></details>
<details><summary>I'm already listed with a Realtor. Can I still talk to you?</summary><p>Yes. You can still talk to us if the house is already listed. <h2 id="foreclosure">Foreclosure and hard situations</h2>
<div class="faq">
<details><summary>Is it too late to sell if I've got a foreclosure sale date?</summary><p>A sale date does not, by itself, mean it is too late. It also does not mean a sale will stop the foreclosure.</p><p>A sale helps only when there is time and the numbers work. We do not promise that foreclosure stops, or anything about your credit. Lender, counselor, cash, and market-sale options are on the <a href="/stop-foreclosure/">stop foreclosure</a> page. For legal advice, use a foreclosure attorney or a HUD-approved counselor at <a href="https://www.hud.gov/counseling">hud.gov/counseling</a>. We are not attorneys.</p></details>
<details><summary>Will selling hurt or help my credit?</summary><p>We will not tell you that selling helps your credit, or that it saves it.</p><p>We do not know what a lender will report. A sale is not a credit-repair product. If you are worried about a foreclosure on your record, a HUD-approved housing counselor or a foreclosure attorney can talk through that. We are neither.</p></details>
<details><summary>Can I sell an inherited house that's still in probate?</summary><p>Inherited houses are a common reason people call. Whether you can sell while probate is still open is a legal question, and we are not answering it here.</p><p class="ph">Placeholder, waiting on Andrew or Taylor and on counsel.
