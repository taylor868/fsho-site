<section class="wrap pad"><h2>A house you received, not a project you planned</h2><p>An inherited house can show up with someone else's furniture still in it, a yard you do not live near, and decisions you did not ask for. You might want to keep it. You might want to be done. This page is for the second feeling.</p><p>You can ask for a no-obligation offer without cleaning the house out or fixing it up. There is no cost to ask. If we are not the right fit, we will say so.</p></section>

An as-is conversation is not a weekend of hauling.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>Make repairs</h3><p>A roof, a floor, or years of postponed work can stay as they are while you ask for an offer.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>Live nearby</h3><p>You can call from another state. <section class="wrap pad"><h2>Inherited is not the same as probate</h2><p>Inherited means the house came to you. Probate is the legal process a court may still be running. If you already have the right to sell and the court work is done, you are in the right place. If you are not sure you are allowed to sign, do not guess. Ask the estate's attorney or the court. We are not attorneys, and we will not tell you that you can sign.</p><p>When you are ready to talk about the house itself, use the form or call.
