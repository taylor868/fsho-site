<section class="wrap pad"><h2>An occupied rental is a landlord problem, not a slogan</h2><p>Selling a house with tenants means people still live there. Rent may be late. The lease may have months left. The house may need work you are tired of funding. You can want out of being a landlord without wanting a fight.</p><p>You can ask for a no-obligation offer while the house is occupied. We do not promise an eviction. We do not promise that tenants will leave. We do not tell you to push anyone out.</p></section>

<section class="alt"><div class="wrap pad"><h2 class="c">What to tell us</h2><div class="grid2"><div class="card"><h3>Who lives there</h3><p>You, a tenant, or nobody. If it is a tenant, say so. You do not have to have them moved out first.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>What you know about the lease</h3><p>Share what you know. We will not invent lease terms, and we will not tell you a lease disappears at closing.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>The condition</h3><p>Repairs, vacancies in other units, or a tired building can stay as they are while you ask. As-is includes the work you do not want to do.</p></div><div class="card"><h3>The property type</h3><p>We buy single-family houses and multi-family properties with 2–4 units. An occupied rental still has to be one of those, in a state on our <a href="/service-areas/">service areas</a> page.</p></div></div></div></section>

<section class="wrap pad"><h2>What a sale does not do</h2><p>Selling does not evict anyone. It does not promise the tenants will cooperate, move, or pay back rent. If you need legal help with a lease, talk with a lawyer. We are not attorneys, and we are not a property manager.</p><p>If the house is empty, say that too.
